_From conceptual model to running database_

== What you built

Starting from a real dataset, you:

* stated its facts and constraints in plain sentences,
* modeled places, companies, facilities, commodities and trade flows, choosing between alternatives at each step,
* implemented the result directly in TypeQL, including a seven-role relation with an optional role and attributes of its own,
* watched the database enforce the model on queries, on writes, at commit, and on changes to the model itself,
* queried by type, by role, polymorphically and recursively,
* queried and documented the model with the same language,
* and worked around what the schema cannot express.

There was no logical design step: the ER diagrams and the TypeQL describe the same model.

== Where next

* Extend the model with more of the Trase dataset: ports of export, sustainability commitments, or ownership shares as an attribute of `ownership`.
* Model a domain of your own. The xref:{page-version}@guides::schema-modeling.adoc[schema modeling guide] covers naming, subtyping versus composition, and constraint design.
* Take xref:{page-version}@academy::index.adoc[TypeDB Academy] for a systematic course in TypeQL, including xref:{page-version}@academy::9-modeling-schemas/index.adoc[modeling schemas].
* Look up any statement or annotation in the xref:{page-version}@typeql-reference::index.adoc[TypeQL reference].
* Ask questions in the https://typedb.com/discord[TypeDB Discord].
