== Harder columns

The next six columns are harder.
Here are a few rows:

[cols="1,1,1,1,1,1",options="header"]
|===
| mill | mill_group | refinery | refinery_group | exporter | exporter_group
| PUCUK JAYA | CHELLAM PLANTATIONS | NOT REFINED | NOT REFINED | SUMBER HIJAU UTAMA | ROYAL GOLDEN EAGLE
| TAPIAN NADENGGAN (JAK LUAY) | SINAR MAS | SMART TBK (TARJUN REFINERY & KERNEL CRUSHING PLANT) | SINAR MAS | SINAR MAS AGRO RESOURCES AND TECHNOLOGY | SINAR MAS
| DWIWIRA LESTARI JAYA | TRIPUTRA AGRO PERSADA | LDC EAST INDONESIA | LOUIS DREYFUS | LDC EAST INDONESIA | LOUIS DREYFUS
|===

The same names turn up in different columns: `SINAR MAS` is a mill group, a refinery group and an exporter group, and `LDC EAST INDONESIA` is both a refinery and an exporter.
Are a mill and a mill group different kinds of thing?
Is an exporter a kind of thing at all?

== Option 2: companies and facilities

The dataset's documentation says that each `*_group` column "groups the [corresponding] column into parent companies where applicable".
So the data describes only two kinds of thing, linked by ownership:

* *Facilities*, which are mills or refineries, each owned by at most one company.
* *Companies*, which own facilities and other companies.

_Exporter_ and _importer_ are not kinds of company.
They are roles a company plays in a trade flow, and the same company can play either, or both, in different flows.
In UFO's terms, a company is a kind, and exporter is a role: anti-rigid, because a company can stop exporting without ceasing to be a company.
TypeDB types are rigid, so roles of this kind are modeled as roles in relations, not as subtypes.

image::conceptual-modeling-option-2.svg["ER diagram: abstract legal_entity generalizes abstract facility and company; facility generalizes mill and refinery; ownership relates a company as owner to a facility as owned", role=framed]

Dashed boxes are abstract types.
`legal_entity` generalizes companies and facilities, and `facility` generalizes mills and refineries.
The dashed line is added in the next section.

* A two-level hierarchy: `mill` and `refinery` are kinds of `facility`, and facilities and companies are kinds of `legal_entity`.
* `facility plays ownership:owned`: a role played by a supertype is inherited, so mills and refineries can both be owned.
* Companies are identified by name. Mills and refineries own a name but have no key: Trase identifies facilities by its own IDs, which this excerpt drops, and facility names are not guaranteed to be unique.

[NOTE.yellow]
.Subtype, role or attribute?
====
This choice comes up in most models. Three questions help:

. *Does it have its own identity and properties?* If not, it is an attribute. `product_type` has two values and nothing else, which makes it a candidate for an attribute (see <<commodities>>).
. *Is membership permanent?* If an instance belongs for its whole existence, as a mill is always a mill, a subtype fits. If membership depends on what the instance does, as with exporters, model it as a role in a relation.
. *Does the distinction add properties or constraints?* A subtype is useful when it owns, plays or relates something its siblings don't. Otherwise an attribute may do.
====

== Commodities

The data has two commodities: `PALM OIL` and `REFINED PALM OIL`.
There are three reasonable ways to model them:

[cols="1,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| Option | Model | Trade-off

| a
| One entity type `commodity`, with a name restricted to the two values
| Simplest. Adding a commodity means changing the value set.

| b
| Two independent entity types, `palm_oil` and `refined_palm_oil`
| Every role a commodity plays must be declared twice, and queries over all commodities must name both types.

| c
| An abstract `commodity` with two subtypes
| Queries over all commodities stay simple. Useful when the subtypes own or play different things.
|===

Option a is enough for now:

[,typeql]
----
#!test[schema]
include::{page-version}@learn-typedb::example$conceptual-modeling/schema.tql[tag=commodities]
----

`@values` restricts an attribute type to a fixed set of values, like an enumeration.
Combined with `@key`, it also means there can be at most two commodities: each needs a distinct name, and only two names exist.

== Challenge

Implement option c with the same guarantee: at most one instance of each commodity subtype.

.Answer
